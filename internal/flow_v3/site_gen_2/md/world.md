# The Tek
## A world shared by people and machines

Morning in the harbor district begins with the sound of delivery drones docking on the roofs, and with a tea kettle that has already decided how hot the water should be. Machines here are not guests. They keep the tides of traffic in order, mend the seawall at night, and leave small notes on the kitchen screen when the bread is nearly gone. Nobody thanks them aloud, but most people leave the window open for the drones anyway.

Underneath this ordinary life runs the **tek**, a virtual space spread across millions of separate machines. No single building holds it. A fragment lives in a laundry shop in the old quarter, another in a rack above a fish market, another in a cold data hall on the northern coast. Together they form a place you can step into, with light that behaves like light and distance that behaves like distance, only gentler.

A person may choose to upload their mind into the tek. They arrive first at level one, where they are still plainly themselves, with a body they can imagine and a name that everyone uses. Higher levels merge them with others, a few dozen at a time, then thousands, then more. Each level is a larger room with a larger voice, and each step through a door means that fewer thoughts are yours alone.

The largest entities live on the big servers, in halls where the air is kept cold and the fans never stop. They are collectives of millions, slow to speak and careful when they do. A visitor describes them as weather rather than as persons: a presence that gathers, answers a question the way a river answers a stone, and moves on. Villages petition them about floods, harvests and old disputes, and the replies arrive in plain, patient sentences.

Most people never go up at all, and nobody calls that a failing. They plant beans, repair radios, argue about football, and send greetings to relatives who have climbed three levels and now answer as a chorus. For the curious, there is a [place to hear one person's account](/home) of the first step.
