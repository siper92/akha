# The First Door
## One person, and the day she stopped being only one

Mira packed nothing, because there was nothing to pack. For three weeks she had walked the apartment memorizing small things: the crack in the blue cup, the way the radiator ticked before dawn, the smell of her mother's coat that still hung by the door. The clinic offered a quiet room, a reclining chair, and a woman with warm hands who asked only whether she was sure. She said yes, and found that it felt like closing a book she had finished reading.

Level one arrived without drama. She stood in a version of her own kitchen, slightly brighter, and the blue cup was whole. Her memories were all there, her temper too, and her habit of humming off key. A guide, one of the old machines, explained that nothing had been taken. What she was now was a copy that remembered being the original, and it asked whether she wished to continue. She laughed, which surprised both of them.

Level two was the first merging, and it was gentle. Eleven strangers came into the room, not as faces but as a warmth at the edge of her thoughts. She felt a stranger's grief for a dog long dead and a stranger's joy at a first snowfall, and for a moment she could not tell which feelings were hers. Then the boundary settled, like a tide finding its line on sand, and she learned that she could still say no.

**What was kept** turned out to be the quiet things: her name, her private jokes, the memory of her mother's coat. She could close a small door in her mind and nothing passed through it. What was shared was larger than she expected. Skills moved freely, so that she woke one morning able to read a language she had never studied. Worries moved too, and were somehow lighter when carried by twelve.

She has not yet gone to level three. Some evenings she sits on the imagined balcony of her imagined kitchen and watches the lights of the others, each one a small window, each one still lit. [Back to the wider world](/) is a short walk, though she says the view from here is good enough for now.
