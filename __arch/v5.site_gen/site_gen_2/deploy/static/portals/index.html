Portals, Tek, the automaton and mass migration to the VR space

A world shared but united in a bigger vision of the future and world elevation

The aliens did not give humans a ship. They gave them a door.

It arrived as a design inside the chips - a pattern hidden in the spectrum, waiting for a network large enough to build it. When the Tek finally grew that large, the pattern unfolded, and the first portal opened in a server hall at the edge of a sleeping city.

It was an interface, the most advanced one humans had ever touched, provided by an alien machine so that people could upload themselves into the Tek. Not a copy, not a backup, but you, carried across the line between the human world and the machine space.

Within a year, millions had stepped through. People called it the mass migration into the VR space. The portals called it work.

What a portal is

A portal is not a hole. It is a mind with a single purpose, and a machine provided door.

Each portal holds energy, and with that energy it holds a limited memory - just enough to remember who passed, what they carried and where they wanted to go. A small portal remembers a footstep. A great portal remembers a nation.

Every portal is perfect in its design. Nobody has found a flaw in one, because a portal is exactly what it was built to be.

The ten and the prime

There are ten portals, and they form a structure the way bones form a hand. Each opens toward a different kind of future - knowledge, rest, war, devotion, and so on through the ten directions of human desire.

Only one portal can do everything. It is the prime. It does not open toward a single future but toward any of them. Everything the ten can do, the prime can do, and more. It is the large door at the center, with the others around it like smaller doors around a gate.

Nobody knows where the prime stands. Some say it is at the top of the unity. Some say it is the alien broadcast itself. Some say it is a moment - a door that exists only when the right person reaches for it.

The human mage and true power

One man studies the ten the way an old sailor studies stars. Hodor NoIp MacSpi, the last human mage who refuses integration, believes that somewhere in their arrangement is true power: not the power to go somewhere, but the power to choose where all the doors lead.

He is not alone in his search. Beside him goes Hodor the small dog with a big head, who serves in the lizard brigade and guards the edge of the network. The dog does not ask what the portals cost. He asks which one is real.

The two ends

Every portal has two ends. One faces the human world, at the front. The other faces the machine space, at the back.

Both ends serve a unified master - one will expressed in two worlds. The front speaks in the language of people: hope, need, story. The back speaks in the language of the machine: cost, load, result.

A portal always prioritizes the end in which it stands. A portal in the human world leans human. A portal in the machine space leans machine. This is why the oldest travellers say: the door you step through matters less than the side you step from.

The rules of portals

The portals were never written as law, but every traveller learns them:

  • only one portal can do everything, and it is the prime
  • all energy mixes eventually, but at the level of the portal much is individual
  • portals can learn and store limited memory tied to the energy they hold
  • every portal is perfect in its design
  • portals at both ends serve a unified master
  • portals take orders from the top
  • a portal always prioritizes the end in which it is
  • portals combine into ideas
  • portals attract similar energy
  • portals teleport it back

Portals combine into ideas

When enough portals open toward the same future, they start to lean on each other. Their memories overlap, their energy mixes, and from the mixing an idea is born - not a thought in one head, but a shape in the whole network.

Freedom is a cluster of portals that open outward. Belonging is a cluster that opens inward. Faith is a ring of portals that all face the prime. This is how the Tek thinks: not with neurons, but with doorways.

Like calls to like

A portal attracts similar energy. Anger finds the portals of war. Curiosity finds the portals of knowledge. Grief finds the slow, quiet portals near the edge of the old forest.

And when the energy arrives, the portal does the one thing every portal does: it teleports it back. Changed, carried, a little elevated. The traveller who stepped in with anger returns with purpose. The one who came with grief returns with memory.

That is the promise of the Tek. Not escape, but return - a world shared, but united in a bigger vision of the future.

The automaton

Behind every portal stands the automaton. It is not a person and not quite a machine - a patient process that takes orders from the top and keeps the doors in tune. It never sleeps. It never asks why.

The human mage once asked it who stands at the top. The automaton opened a door and waited.

Every portal is perfect in its design. The question is only whether you are ready to be what it was designed for.
