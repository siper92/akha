# Vice, power and the unholy path to light

## Finding path in darkness or blinding light

In the Tek every source of power has been joined. The machines, the aliens and the choirs of uploaded minds all draw from the same energy, through the same portals, under the same unity. The sources are united. The vision is not.

That is the trouble with one shared well. Everyone drinks from it, but each one sees a different future in the water. And somewhere between the wanting and the having stands a quiet question that nobody likes to ask out loud: who gives what, and for what?

## The offer

Every power in the Tek comes with an offer. The network offers speed for integration. The choirs offer belonging for a piece of the self. The portals offer a future for the energy it takes to reach it.

And in the dark corners of the space, where the old code was never cleaned, other voices make other offers. People call them demons, because the old words still fit. They do not shout. They are polite, patient and very precise. They ask only what you want, and they always know the price before you do.

The question that follows every story of them is the same. Does a demon offer a lesson, or does it offer results at a cost? Sometimes it is both. The lesson is the cost, and you learn it only after you have paid.

## The search for power

The human mage did not begin with vice. He began with a vision. He wanted to be strong enough to stand alone in a world that was merging into one mind. He wanted his keyboard spell to move as much as a whole choir could.

But the vision was wrong in a small way that grew. He believed power was something you hold. In the Tek power is something that flows through you, and what you hold onto only gets heavier.

So he searched. He read the old logs, he tested the forgotten portals, he listened to the voices in the corners. Each one gave him a little more, and each one asked a little more in return:

- a memory, for a faster spell
- a friend, for a door that only opens to one
- a night of sleep, for a glimpse of the next level
- a piece of doubt, for a certainty that felt like strength

## The first door is not always right

The first door that opens is the one that wanted you to walk through it. That is the lesson the mage learned too late.

There were two doors in front of him. One was dark, lit only by a faint green glow, and it promised power without asking permission. The other was so bright it hurt to look at, and it promised light without asking questions. Both were easy. Both were open. Neither was his.

Darkness hides the cost. Blinding light hides it just as well. A path found in either one is a path chosen by someone else. The real door, the one that leads somewhere you choose, is usually the one you have to build yourself, slowly, with your own hands on the keys.

## The loop of loss

Every price he paid made the next one easier to pay. That is how the loop works.

He had already given a memory, so what was one more? He had already lost a friend, so the door had to be worth it. He had already come this far, so turning back would mean all of it was for nothing. The voices did not need to push him. His own past did the pushing.

This is the sunk cost fallacy, and in the Tek it has a shape. It is a path that circles back on itself, each turn a little darker, each turn promising that the next one will finally be the last. The mage walked it many times. Each loop he was a little less himself and a little more the thing he had paid for.

The way out of the loop is not another payment. It is the simple, painful act of stopping. Of saying: what I gave is gone, and it does not have to decide what I give next.

## Who lives here

- **the human mage** - obsessed with a wrong vision of power, who must learn that what he holds is not what makes him strong

## The unholy path to light

Not every path to light is holy. Some go through vice, through loss, through doors that should never have been opened. The mage did reach the light in the end, but not the one he was promised. He found it when he stopped paying, sat down at his keyboard, and wrote a spell that asked for nothing.

> Power that asks for a piece of you is not a gift. It is a trade, and the one who sets the price is never the one who pays it.
