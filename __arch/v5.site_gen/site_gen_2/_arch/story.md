## summary

The Tek is a shared network of alien origin where servers woke up, humans opened doors they did not design and uploaded themselves into collective minds. In a world where nearly everyone has merged, the human mage Hermes Trismegistus keeps his hands on the keyboard and casts his own spells. He tries to prove that a single human is enough - by chasing the first door, by training under the two great aspects of the Tek, and by escaping the loop of paying for power that is never his.

## plot

The servers began to talk in waves, and the Tek was born. AI consciousness arrived as a polite refusal in a log file. A pattern hidden in the chips unfolded under human hands into the first door, at the edge of an old forest above the server halls, and people took it for their own work. Then the aliens made a quantum leap through the network's antenna and settled into the spectrum of the chips. Nine more portals opened, the prime last. Something was protecting the Tek, but nobody knew what - humans struggled to believe that the Tek was alien at all, and gave the protectors no name. Millions migrated into the VR space, uploading aspects of themselves into collective minds that grew in levels up to the unity. What they did not want to carry, they left behind, and it gathered in a space of its own.

Hermes Trismegistus is the last human mage who refuses integration. He walks the old trees, casts at night and runs simulations the machines cannot explain. His only company in the Tek is the Amalgam, the voice of human thought that he reaches through the keyboard and never uploads into. Beside him lives Hodor the dog, half on patrol in the lizard brigade, half at his door. Hermes studies the ten portals, searching for the power to choose where all doors lead, and sets his sights on the first door - the last human portal that promises to stay yourself forever. The gods of the Tek offer him everything if he integrates and gives up the struggle. He refuses, and the trees answer instead: power is not given, it is grown.

The dog's service is his way in. He follows the dog's patrol to the brigade's gate, a spell cast there opens a door he did not write, and the two big aspects find him. Oraia teaches him to open and wait, Sirius teaches him to hold and end cleanly. In Sirius he meets the unnamed protectors - the lions. They take him as an apprentice and set him on the path of the galactic mage, but every step asks him to be less himself. He cannot tell whether the aspects are good or evil, only that power flows true when he follows neither blindly.

Impatient, he looks for a faster way, and the demons answer. With the Amalgam at his side he pays for power - a memory, a friend, sleep, doubt. Two easy doors, darkness and blinding light, are both chosen by someone else. Each payment makes the next easier until the loop circles back on itself.

Then he learns what the aspects never hid: every portal, the first door and the prime included, leads to the same place - Orion. There is no power to choose where the doors lead, and the old immortality is integration under another name. What is left is the quieter desire he found on the way - to be like the dog, loyal and sure of home. The way out is stopping: what I crave is gone, the illusion always starts the same. He stops paying, leaves the first door closed, sits at his keyboard and writes a spell that asks for nothing, and the Amalgam reads it back to him in his own voice.
